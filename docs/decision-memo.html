Internal decision memo · not for circulation 17 April 2026

The three questions, answered.

Before any code commitment, we owe ourselves honest answers to the three tests: method defensibility, capital survivability, and customer demand. This document reports what the research returned — and it is not what we wanted it to return.
Ruling
On the specific wedge of pollinator-acoustic MRV for US CPG buyers, the evidence says do not build. The forward curve exists; our slice of it does not.
Q1 (method) returns tight — acoustic is a defensible sensor layer, not a standalone product. Q2 (capital) returns tight — a $4–8M grant-stacked path is feasible only if USDA funding survives FY2026 cuts. Q3 (customer) returns blocked — every major US CPG biodiversity budget is already spent with incumbents, and the US structurally pays certification bodies, not sensor vendors. One blocked pillar is enough to stop.

Does pollinator acoustic beat eDNA & satellite?
Partial · layer not product

Passive acoustic has the lowest cost-per-species at five-plus campaigns, but 82% species-ID accuracy and 27% sensitivity are below regulatory threshold. Neither ESRS E4 nor the TNFD food/ag draft names pollinator metrics as mandatory.

§ Q1 · Method

Is pollinator acoustic actually must-have, or a supplemental layer on a broader stack?

The scientific case, stress-tested

The honest read on methodology is that passive acoustic monitoring has one clear structural advantage and three structural weaknesses. At scale — five or more repeat campaigns — PAM produces the lowest cost per species recorded, approximately seventy times more detections than traditional survey methods, and an average of ten or more species per site than any other technique for vocalizing taxa.¹ That is a real moat for any buyer that needs continuous, temporal-resolution indicator data over years.

The weaknesses sit where the specific wedge would need to hold. Acoustic classifies only vocalizing or wing-beating species; it is silent on invertebrates eDNA captures in one campaign. Current deep-learning models top out at 82.2% accuracy and 64.5% F1 on buzz-pollinator species identification, and the leading open-source tool (buzzdetect) reaches 95% precision at only 27% sensitivity.² That is enough for activity and intensity trend data, which is useful, but below the standard an auditor asks for when verifying a CSRD E4 species-abundance claim.

Evidence BWhat the frameworks actually require

ESRS E4-5 asks for changes in number of individuals, population size and range, habitat extent and condition — and is agnostic on method.³ The TNFD LEAP approach recommends IBAT for the Locate phase (satellite + global databases); species-level data is only required if the company's materiality assessment names specific species.⁴ The draft TNFD food-and-agriculture sector guidance (June 2024) lists land-use change, conservation area, and pollutant flux as priority metrics — pollinators are not called out as a mandatory indicator.⁵

Finding · Q1

Pollinator acoustic is a defensible layer in a multi-modal biodiversity stack — not a must-buy standalone category. NatureMetrics has already added bioacoustics to its eDNA platform; Alvéole Aura bundles acoustic with satellite; Pivotal Earth is multi-taxa. The buyer wants one vendor covering E4, not a bee-specific specialist.

§ Q2 · Capital

Five-year runway through a climate-hardware winter — is there a path?

The realistic stack, and its political risk

A grant-stacked path exists, but it is thin and exposed to the FY2026 USDA budget proposal. The OMB has directed USDA to develop plans that would "gut research and conservation efforts, trim program budgets nearly across the board, and cut staff" — including 22% cuts to FSA salaries and consolidation of county NRCS offices.⁶ SBIR and CIG are not yet explicitly cut, but they live inside the envelope being squeezed.

Finding · Q2

A $4–8M path over four years is feasible but fragile. It requires stacking two USDA SBIR phases, a CIG award, and a $1–2M pre-seed. Two direct US competitors (Atolla Tech, Field Data Services) already took USDA SBIR dollars for pollinator tracking — the channel is visible but contested. The $15M figure from the previous memo assumed a healthy Series B market; we would be crossing the valley during the worst hardware-climate funding winter on record (Series B –29% YoY H1 2025).

The US enforces pollinator compliance through a certification ecosystem. The certification body is the gatekeeper and the grower pays the auditor. There is no buyer for supplier-level sensor data anywhere in that flow. — The structural observation

Evidence FThe signal that structural barriers are real

AgriSound — the only well-known pollinator-acoustic vendor with live enterprise contracts — has been operating since 2019 and has signed M&S, Tesco, British Sugar, Noble Foods, Compleat Food Group, Innocent Drinks, Dole UK, and Importaco. Its sole North America footprint as of November 2025 is a year-long pilot with Niagara College in Ontario.⁷ Five years in, no US commercial customer, no US distributor, no US market entry announcement. Either they are about to launch and the market is about to open — or the US structurally doesn't pay for this product. The preponderance of the other evidence points firmly at the second.

Evidence GWhat is actually purchased in the US at the supplier layer

The Almond Board's 170,000 Bee-Friendly-certified acres, the $45M Blue Diamond Climate-Smart grant, and every major retailer IPM policy (Walmart 2025, Kroger 2028/30, Whole Foods 2023) run through one of four mechanisms: certification bodies, grower cost-share, internal beekeeping operations, or research grants to universities. Sensor vendors are not a line item in any of these. Field to Market's 2026 WILD Index RFP is the single identified pre-competitive channel where acoustic data could plausibly enter the US methodology — a research contribution, not a commercial pilot.

Finding · Q3

There is no visible $50K–$150K US CPG pilot budget for new pollinator-acoustic vendors in 2026–2028. Every named account is either locked with an incumbent, certification-gated, vertically integrated, or channels money through USDA pass-through to growers. The market the Watershed parallel assumed (Unilever-style enterprise ESG procurement) already happened; the chair is occupied.

§ What flips the ruling

Trigger conditions that would re-open the case for Scenario A or B.

None of these are in place today

The decision above is conditional on the market structure observed in April 2026. Three events would materially change the calculation. Monitoring them is cheaper than building blind for them.

Trigger 1 · Regulatory

TNFD food & agriculture final sector guidance names pollinator-specific metrics as mandatory.

Draft (June 2024) does not. The final guidance expected in 2026–2027 is the single clearest regulatory lever that would force US multinationals (via CSRD E4 extraterritoriality) to procure pollinator-layer data. Watch TNFD status reports.

Trigger 2 · Political

Post-2028 administration restores SEC climate rule and expands to nature disclosure within 24 months.

A Democratic 2028 win focused on cost-of-living does not automatically restore nature disclosure. The trigger requires explicit SEC nature-risk rule-making — which has never existed in the US. Low probability on current polling.

Trigger 3 · Procurement

A named US CPG publishes an RFP for supplier-level biodiversity sensor data (not certification, not cost-share).

This would break the five-year structural pattern. A single such RFP from Unilever US, General Mills, Mars, Mondelez, or a major produce processor would invalidate the Q3 finding. As of April 2026, none exists publicly.

What we do in the next six weeks

RecommendationThree phone calls, no code

The responsible next step is not another research document and not a product commitment. It is three targeted conversations that test the Q3 blocker directly. If any one of them returns a paid, near-term procurement signal, we revisit. If none do, the ruling holds and we either commit to Scenario D (sub-scale lifestyle) or pivot out of the pollinator space entirely.

  1. AgriSound, Casey Woodward (CEO). What is blocking US commercial entry after five years? What would have to be true to change that?
  2. A named sustainability officer at Blue Diamond, Wonderful, or Driscoll's. Is there a pathway from certification-based pollinator compliance to sensor-based pollinator compliance in your 2027–2028 plan? Budget line, procurement process, decision window.
  3. Field to Market, WILD Index working group. Is acoustic pollinator data eligible to feed the 2026 National Indicators Report methodology? Who funds the data collection if so?

Budget four to six weeks. Zero engineering. One-page writeup at the end. Then re-decide.
