The question: given the evidence in the synthesis doc, what should we build to create the most value? The method: don't start from our codebase or our instincts. Start from what has been proven to produce $100M+ outcomes in analog markets, check which analogs map to the beekeeper universe, then work backward to the specific first moves. If the evidence doesn't support a venture path, the analysis says so — without rescuing the thesis from its own data.
Preface

You proposed that "build users now" and "bet on a future catalyst" might be the same move, not two moves. I didn't want to assume that was right just because it was yours. The research below was designed to break the idea before it got written up.

The short version of what came back: the combined approach does hold up — but not as a Waze-style consumer network. The evidence points at a specific, narrower shape: a professional-grade data vendor play modeled on Floodbase, where a beekeeper tool is the data-acquisition layer and parametric insurers / reinsurers are the economic buyer. This is different from what I said in the synthesis and I want to explain exactly what changed my mind and what could still kill it.

Chapter 01 Start from the end
Four analogs that created the value we want to create.
Each of these companies built a user-contributed or sensor-contributed data network in an adjacent domain, and each of them produced an outcome between $250M and $1.1B. The question isn't whether the model works — it obviously does — it's which analog our situation maps to, and which it doesn't.

The numerator that matters is not beekeeper count — it's insurance-grade data coverage of crops where pollination peril is real. ~2,300,000 non-BeeHero hives distributed across 5M+ pollinated US acres is a rich enough dataset to produce the statistical basis required for parametric triggers, if harvested rigorously.

Finding 01
The value isn't in the number of users. It's in the coverage of pollination events. Our universe produces enough pollination events to be insurance-relevant — the bottleneck is rigor, not scale.
Chapter 03 Catalyst map
The catalysts that could inflect the category.
A Floodbase-shaped data vendor is a slow build. It only pays if a catalyst creates mandatory demand for pollination-peril data. Here are the catalysts actually on the board, with honest probability estimates anchored to specific evidence.
Catalyst board
2026–2027 · high likelihood
Arbol or Descartes launches a pollination-peril parametric product.
Probability · 55%
Arbol-Pollen Systems partnership launched Feb 2026 already uses satellite + GIS triggers. Bee-sensor triggers are the obvious next step. Either uses BeeHero data exclusively (kills this play), or an open data layer (validates it). Watching which way this goes is the cheapest signal we can buy.
2027–2028 · moderate likelihood
Second 30–40%+ commercial colony loss event.
Probability · 35–40%
2024–2025 losses were 62% commercial. Varroa amitraz-resistance, DWV-A/B, acute paralysis virus all remain structurally present. A second bad year forces USDA RMA to either expand API or green-light private parametric partners, and forces CPG buyers to require verification.
2027–2029 · uncertain
TNFD v2 sector guidance names pollinator metrics.
Probability · 20–25%
TNFD transition-plan guidance released Nov 2025, ISSB building a nature standard on TNFD architecture. EU Pollinator Monitoring Scheme methodology completed Oct 2024. Pollinator-specific metric adoption is plausible but not scheduled. Would bring enterprise MRV budget into the category.
2026–2028 · unclear
Federal parametric crop insurance passes Congress.
Probability · 15–25%
US Senators introduced a bill to add parametric products to the Federal Crop Insurance Program. If passed, opens federal subsidy for private parametric providers and creates regulatory admission for sensor-triggered products. Time-dependent on Congressional calendar.
2027+ · baseline
State-level parametric expansion follows NY.
Probability · 60%
NY enacted parametric-specific insurance law Dec 2024. California, Florida, and North Dakota (top three API enrollment states) are all plausible follow-ups. Lower-bar catalyst — gives private parametric products a clearer state-DOI pathway without federal action.
Any time · always on
Strategic approach from General Mills / Rabobank / BeeHero itself.
Probability · low but non-zero
General Mills is a BeeHero investor. Rabobank is a BeeHero investor. MS&AD (Japanese insurer) is a BeeHero investor. If we build a credible non-BeeHero data asset, each has a reason to acquire rather than compete. Not a plan, but a scenario.
Finding 02
At least one catalyst in the 2026–2028 window has probability above 50%. Stacking the top three, the probability that at least one catalyst materially reshapes the category within 24 months is roughly 75–80%. That's a bettable distribution — not a sure thing, but not wishful thinking either.
Chapter 04 The specific build
What the backward induction actually prescribes.
Starting from Floodbase as the reference and working back three years: here is the sequence that fits our stack, capital, and the catalyst window. Not a roadmap. A hypothesis about the shape.
Build sequence
00Now–Month 6

Validate, don't build.

Before rebuilding product, test whether the buyer actually exists. Two classes of conversation, no code.

  • Three parametric-MGA conversations (Arbol, Floodbase, Descartes) on appetite for bee-sensor triggers and what data rigor they'd require
  • Two reinsurer conversations (Swiss Re Corp Solutions, MS&AD via BeeHero introduction) on pollination-peril appetite
  • Three non-BeeHero commercial beekeeper conversations on what data they'd be willing to share and what they'd want in return
Pass signal
At least one MGA says "if you bring X data at Y rigor, we'd co-develop a pilot product." Without this, the rest of the sequence is speculation.
01Month 6–18

Ship the beekeeper tool, but build it as a data-acquisition system.

Existing Flutter + BLE + Firebase/Supabase stack becomes a free-or-cheap app for sideliners and non-BeeHero commercial operators. Premium features ($10–30/month) fund operations. The architecture matters more than the feature set: every user event must be designed to produce insurance-grade data.

  • Sensor protocol designed for provenance (cryptographic signatures, timestamp integrity, sensor identity)
  • Phone-only mode to onboard users without hardware (Strava parallel)
  • Explicit data-licensing consent flow that beekeepers sign at onboarding
  • Scientific-advisory partnership with Project Apis m. or BIP for methodology validation
Pass signal
1,000+ active users producing data that passes a third-party statistical rigor check. MGA conversations from Phase 0 converting to LOIs.
02Month 18–36

Co-develop the first parametric pilot product.

Partner with one MGA (Arbol or Floodbase itself) to launch a small-scale pilot — one crop, one region, bounded limits. Not a national product. A single blueberry co-op in Washington, or a single almond sub-region. The objective is the first bound policy using our data as the trigger, not revenue.

  • $3–5M seed round from climate/insurtech VCs (Lowercarbon, EIF, Collaborative Fund — Floodbase's backers)
  • Reinsurer pre-commitment on the pilot structure
  • Scientific paper published on pollination-performance-to-yield correlation using our data
Pass signal
First bound policy triggered by our data feed. Doesn't have to be big. Has to exist and pay out correctly.
03Month 36+

Scale if a catalyst hits. Hold if not.

By month 36 we know whether the catalyst materialized (second colony crisis, TNFD v2, federal parametric approval). If yes, scale the data feed into adjacent crops and geographies. If no, we still have a real business — a parametric data vendor with a niche product and a defensible data moat — just at a slower trajectory.

  • Scale case: expand MGA partnerships, hire actuarial + insurance compliance, $15–20M Series A
  • Hold case: optimize for profitability at $5–10M ARR, long-accumulate data, become attractive acquisition target for strategic (Bayer, Syngenta, Rabobank) over 5–10 years
Exit condition
The Climate Corp pattern: 5–10 years of rigorous data makes the asset valuable to a buyer with distribution. Not our ceiling — our floor if the venture outcome doesn't hit.
Chapter 05 Kill conditions
What would break this specific thesis.
Each of these is a named failure mode, not a hypothetical. If any one lands, the build-sequence above is wrong and we should stop spending cycles on it.
Stress tests
Break 01
Phase 0 MGA conversations return flat "satellite + weather is enough."
Check by: Month 6. If Arbol, Floodbase, and Descartes all say hive-sensor triggers add marginal accuracy but not enough to justify onboarding new data rigor, the underlying buyer doesn't exist yet and the Floodbase analogy doesn't transfer.
Break 02
BeeHero announces an insurance product with Arbol / MS&AD / reinsurer partner.
Check by: Any time. If BeeHero directly occupies the parametric-data-vendor seat with exclusive data, the non-BeeHero niche we're betting on shrinks to near-zero. Their MS&AD investor signals this is already in motion.
Break 03
Non-BeeHero commercial beekeepers won't share data.
Check by: Month 12. The ApisProtect pattern — beekeepers resist paying for and contributing to sensor data platforms — could simply repeat. If we can't get 500+ real active users in year one, the data asset never accumulates.
Break 04
None of the catalysts materialize in 36 months.
Check by: Month 36. If no second colony crisis, no TNFD v2 pollinator metrics, no federal parametric bill, no state-level expansion, the business remains a niche data vendor with low growth. Not zero, but not a venture outcome.
Break 05
Climate/insurtech funding winter deepens.
Check by: Late 2026 VC deployment data. Floodbase raised $5M in Feb 2025; Arbol raised $60M in April 2024. If 2026–2027 sees insurtech funding contract further, the $3–5M seed required for Phase 2 may not clear.
Break 06
Data rigor falls short of insurance-grade.
Check by: Month 18. Sensor data from consumer-grade hardware has well-known noise floors. If peer-reviewed validation of our data against ground-truth pollination outcomes fails to hit statistical significance, no MGA will bind a policy on it.
Chapter 06 What changed from the synthesis
Two claims I made last doc that this research updates.
For transparency. Not hedging — actual corrections based on the new data.
Corrections

Update 01 · I said a data-oracle path required insurance DNA we don't have. Floodbase disproves that. Two Yale master's students with climate science backgrounds, starting from a Google Earth Engine school project, now have Swiss Re as a preferred partner. The path is long but it exists for non-insurance teams. This moves "neutral oracle" from Option C (high-variance long shot) to a specific shape with a proven playbook.

Update 02 · I underweighted your combined-approach insight. I was thinking of the user-acquisition play and the catalyst bet as separate options. They're actually the same play expressed across two time horizons: the user layer is the data-acquisition mechanism, the insurer layer is the economic buyer, and the catalyst is what makes the buyer show up in size. Strava Metro and Floodbase are two variants of that same structure. I should have seen that earlier.

What didn't change: the verdict against a Waze-scale consumer network (beekeeper denominator too small), the verdict that Beewise / BeeHero / Alvéole own their respective seats, and the lifestyle-ceiling finding for sideliner SaaS as a standalone play.

Closing Recommendation, honest shape

The strongest answer the evidence supports.

Given everything in this analysis and the synthesis before it, here is the shape that has the most evidence behind it:

Build a beekeeper app that is also, in its architecture, a parametric-insurance-grade pollination data vendor. The users get a real tool. The data gets harvested to insurance standards from day one. The buyer is a parametric MGA or reinsurer, not the beekeeper. The catalyst you're betting on — and the evidence says at least one catalyst in 24 months is 75–80% likely — creates the moment when the data becomes acquisition-relevant or category-defining.

This is not a recommendation to start building tomorrow. It is a recommendation to spend the next six months running Phase 0: three MGA conversations, two reinsurer conversations, three non-BeeHero commercial beekeeper conversations. Each of those conversations is cheaper than a week of engineering. If Phase 0 returns a yes from even one MGA, the shape is validated and the build is justified. If it returns no from all of them, the thesis is wrong and we know without burning code.

That's the most honest "what to build for the most value" the evidence supports. It respects what you pointed out — that users now and catalyst later are the same bet — while grounding it in a specific analog (Floodbase) rather than in conviction.
