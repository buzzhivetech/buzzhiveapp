Topic 01 01

State of play. Where six weeks of analysis has landed us.

Open the meeting by acknowledging the journey was longer and more winding than originally planned, and explain why. Arrive at the clean one-sentence conclusion. Do not defend past positions.
Talking points

Set the frame honestly: we went into this thinking we were building a commercial beekeeper-facing app. Six weeks of independent research, three reversed theses, and one academic finding that required a full rethink got us to a different answer. It is a better answer, but the board should know it is different.

  • The question we were really trying to answer. What is the highest-expected-value bet this team — our skills, network, market knowledge, and capital position — can plausibly win? The existing stack is relevant only to the extent it accelerates that bet; it is not the starting point.
  • Why that distinction matters. "What can we build on our stack" is a constrained-optimization question and produces rationalized answers. "What bet wins on merit for a team like ours" is the honest question, and the answer has to survive independent of any sunk code.
  • The method that worked. Forward-looking structural analysis (what durable forces are actually moving) combined with backward-looking competitive analysis (what categories are already filled). Either alone would have led us wrong.
  • The finding that changed everything. Honeybees are classified as livestock, not biodiversity. The "beewashing" critique is now regulatory and procurement-level, not just academic. This invalidates a honeybee-only biodiversity product but clarifies what the real product is.
  • Where we are today. We have a candidate wedge that survives both the forward and backward tests, four plausible buyer categories, and a six-week validation plan that costs ~$5K and tells us whether to build.
The one-sentence state of play
The highest-expected-value bet available to this team is to build the neutral data infrastructure layer for the crop-pollination event — managed hive telemetry plus native-pollinator bioacoustics plus landscape phenology — sold to multiple convergent buyers. It is the right bet on merit; the existing stack happens to accelerate a portion of it, which is a tailwind but not the reason we're choosing it.
Ask · no action required yet

Tonight is for alignment on the frame, not approval of the plan. If anyone on the board has a material disagreement with the one-sentence state of play, flag it now so we address it before the harder asks later in the agenda.

Topic 02 02

The strategic reframe. From honeybee app to pollination-event data layer.

Explain the positioning shift in plain language. The words matter — the wrong framing will have the board still thinking we are a beekeeper app and then be confused by everything downstream.
Talking points

The reframe is not a pivot away from what we have built — it is picking the right bet on merit, and asking what (if anything) of the existing work accelerates it. Some of our BLE and cloud work happens to fit. That is a tailwind, not the justification.

  • The old frame. "A mobile app for hobbyist and sideliner beekeepers, monetized by subscription." Ceiling: $1–5M ARR. No venture outcome. Confirmed by every comparable company (BroodMinder, HiveTracks, ApisProtect).
  • The new frame. "A neutral data infrastructure layer for the pollination event that serves four convergent enterprise buyers: parametric insurance MGAs, CPG sustainability teams, biodiversity consultancies, and growers." Ceiling: $50–250M ARR if two buyer segments convert. Venture-plausible.
  • Why this shape and not another. Climate Corp, Waze, Floodbase, Alvéole — every durable data-company outcome of the last 15 years was built one abstraction above the initial product. The asset at exit was always data + relationships, not the consumer-facing app.
  • What carries forward from what we've built. Team skill, domain knowledge, beekeeper network, and a working BLE-to-cloud data path. The BLE stack itself is useful if it fits the managed-pollinator data layer; if Phase 0 reveals that partner-sourced or reference hardware wins on rigor or unit economics, we use that instead. We are not married to the code. We are married to value creation.
  • What this does to our identity. We are not selling to beekeepers anymore. We are selling to insurers, brands, consultants, and growers. Beekeepers become instrumented users — the data-acquisition mechanism, not the revenue.
The one-line framing for the board
We are Floodbase for pollination — a scientific data vendor whose technology is deployed with beekeepers and whose revenue comes from the institutions downstream who need to price, insure, and verify pollination risk.
Ask

Does the board accept the positioning shift from "beekeeper app" to "pollination-event data infrastructure"? If not, what specifically would you change about the frame? We need alignment on this before we discuss what to build.

Topic 04 04

The wedge. What we'd actually build, for whom, in what order.

Make the abstract concrete. Show the product shape, the build sequence, and the buyer sequence. The board needs to be able to picture what year one looks like.
The product shape

A continuous-monitoring data platform, instrumented at pollination sites (commercial almond orchards, blueberry fields, sunflower acreage, urban pollinator installations), producing a unified per-site record that multiple enterprise buyers can license, query, and integrate.

Layer 01
Managed hive telemetry
Our existing BLE stack. Weight, temperature, activity, brood condition. Beachhead layer — already in production.
Layer 02
Native pollinator acoustics
Passive bioacoustic devices (partner-sourced — AgriSound or Wilder Sensing) detecting native pollinator presence and diversity.
Layer 03
Landscape phenology
Satellite + weather API integration for bloom timing, floral density, temperature stress. No hardware. Partner data.
Layer 04
Outcome data (year 2+)
Yield, set rate, crop quality — pulled from grower records under data-sharing agreements. This is what turns the record into an insurance-grade trigger.

The convergent buyers. One dataset, four independent revenue surfaces. This is the structural asymmetry that makes the wedge venture-plausible — we only need two of the four to convert for the business to work.

  • Parametric insurance MGAs (Arbol, Floodbase, Descartes, eventually Swiss Re Corp Solutions) — buying defensible data to price pollination-peril triggers. Precedent: Floodbase sells flood data to the same buyers. Market: $3–8B parametric ag insurance, growing 15% annually.
  • Biodiversity consultancies (Alvéole, Biodiversity Lab, future spinouts) — buying continuous site-level data to deliver TNFD/CSRD/GRESB reports under their own brand. Precedent: consulting firms have always been the first to pay for defensible data layers they can't build themselves.
  • CPG sustainability teams (Unilever, General Mills, Nestlé regenerative-ag programs) — buying supplier-linked pollination data as part of MRV on sourcing commitments. Slower to buy, larger contracts when they do.
  • Growers and beekeepers — the instrumented layer. Not a paid customer segment at first. Pay-in-kind via data access, seat costs absorbed by enterprise buyers.
The line to repeat to the board
Our user layer is how we acquire proprietary data that no one else has. The institutional layer is where we are paid for it. The regulatory layer is what makes the institutions buy at scale. Each leg supports the next. None of them work alone — but together they make the business durable.
Ask

Does the board agree with the four-layer product shape and the four-buyer monetization thesis? Specifically: is anyone unconvinced that two of the four buyers would plausibly convert? Name the weakest buyer in the stack so we can pressure-test it in Phase 0.

Topic 05 05

Phase 0 validation. Six weeks, twelve calls, one go/no-go.

This is the approval topic. Everything before it was framing. Walk the board through the cost, the deliverables, and the decision that comes out the other end.
The plan

Before committing any engineering, run twelve targeted conversations designed to confirm or kill the thesis. Each category of call is designed to kill a specific part of the wedge. A pass is all twelve surface signal; a kill is any category returning unanimous "no appetite" or "already solved."

Week 1–2
Insurance axis
3 parametric-MGA conversations (Arbol · Floodbase · Descartes) + 2 reinsurer conversations (Swiss Re Corp Solutions · MS&AD) on appetite for bee-sensor triggers, data rigor required, willingness to pay.
~$0
Week 2–3
Beekeeper axis
3 non-BeeHero commercial beekeeper conversations on data-sharing terms, what they would want in return, whether sensor deployment is tractable at 1,000+ hive scale.
~$500 travel
Week 3–4
Biodiversity axis
2 conversations with native-pollinator acoustic vendors (AgriSound · Polliknow) on data interoperability and OEM partnership. 1 conversation with a nature-credit methodology shop (Plan Vivo · Verra) on landscape data standards.
~$0
Week 4–5
Consultancy axis
1–2 conversations with biodiversity consultancies (Noah Wilson-Rich / Biodiversity Lab; ideally one additional spinout if one emerges) on what data they would license under a white-label platform.
~$0
Week 5–6
Founder synthesis
Write a go/no-go memo. Either: (a) commit to 6-month MVP build with named anchor buyers, (b) pivot to a narrower bet, or (c) kill the thesis and return capital.
—

Total Phase 0 cost. ~$5,000 out-of-pocket plus founder time. No hiring, no hardware commitments, no vendor lock-in. Cheaper than a single week of engineering on the wrong product.

What Phase 0 is explicitly not. It is not a sales cycle. It is not a fundraise. It is not a partnership negotiation. It is twelve diagnostic conversations designed to produce one decision: do we build, or do we not.

Ask · action required

Approve Phase 0 as scoped: six weeks, twelve conversations, ~$5K budget, decision memo as deliverable. Also: ask each board member to flag any warm intros they can provide into the call list (especially MGAs, reinsurers, and CPG sustainability officers).

Topic 06 06

Kill conditions. The outcomes that would make us stop.

Every board expects the upside pitch. Fewer get the downside discipline. Present the kill conditions before asking for approval — it demonstrates honest skepticism and builds credibility for the asks that follow.
Kill signals
Kill · Insurance
0 of 3 MGAs express appetite for pollination-peril data, or all 3 say "we would only buy from an entity with a peer-reviewed methodology already published."
Then: the insurance buyer is out. Re-scope to 3-buyer thesis; pressure-test whether consultancies + CPG + grower alone is venture-scale.
Kill · Native acoustic
Both native-pollinator acoustic vendors refuse data interoperability, or their licensing terms make the integrated product unit-economically impossible.
Then: we cannot deliver the integrated record. Either build the acoustic classifier ourselves (18–24 months, wrong team) or kill the thesis.
Kill · Beekeeper
3 of 3 commercial beekeepers say "no, we would never share raw hive data with a vendor regardless of terms."
Then: the data-acquisition layer is broken at the source. No amount of demand downstream fixes this.
Kill · Regulation
Between now and end of Phase 0: CSRD scope rolled back, SEC climate rule formally withdrawn with no state-level replacement, or TNFD adoption flatlines below 2025 levels.
Then: the compliance-buyer catalyst recedes by 2–4 years. Runway required to reach revenue doubles.
Kill · Consultancy
Noah and any second consultancy both already have a preferred data platform under contract, or neither is willing to discuss licensing terms.
Then: the fastest-to-revenue buyer is closed. Phase 0 shifts to testing whether insurance alone is enough.
Kill · Founder-fit
After Phase 0, the honest answer to "do we have the scientific credibility to be taken seriously as a data vendor" is no — and no co-founder with that credibility is willing to join.
Then: we are the wrong team for this wedge. The plan is right; we are not. Return capital or pivot into an adjacent lane where technical-founder profile is sufficient.

Any one of these kills the current thesis. Two or more and we should return capital to the board. Zero and we proceed to an MVP build with named anchor buyers, which becomes a separate board approval in Q3.

Ask

Does the board accept these as the pre-committed kill conditions? Are there additional kill conditions any member would add? The value of pre-committing to kill conditions is that we don't rationalize past them six months from now.

Topic 08 08

Open questions. Things I do not have an answer to.

End with what you don't know. Naming uncertainty is more credible than pretending it isn't there, and the board's judgment on these questions is more valuable than their judgment on the things you already have answers to.
Open questions
Q 01 · Capital horizon
"If Phase 0 converts to a go, do we have a credible path to a $2–4M seed round on a scientific-data-vendor pitch — or are we still fundraising as an app company?"
Q 02 · Scientific credibility
"Floodbase's moat was a Nature cover and Yale climate-science bona fides. What is our equivalent? If the honest answer is 'none yet,' what is the minimum viable version — a co-founder hire, a published methodology paper, a lab partnership?"
Q 03 · Patience vs. speed
"Alvéole took twelve years to get to Aura. Floodbase took eight years to get to Swiss Re. Are we building for a 2029–2031 compliance catalyst, or do we need revenue signal within 18 months? The answer changes product shape and runway."
Q 04 · Stack discipline
"We are not married to the current code — we are married to value creation. That said, carrying forward the pieces that genuinely accelerate the wedge is real leverage. Is the board aligned that Phase 0 should evaluate existing stack components on merit (does this hardware pass insurance-grade rigor? does this schema fit the partner APIs?) rather than on preservation?"
Q 05 · Plan B
"If Phase 0 kills the primary thesis, the right plan B is the highest-EV bet this team can make next — not the smallest thing we can salvage from the current code. Is the board aligned that plan B gets the same value-first treatment (new analysis, honest ruling) rather than a reflexive retreat to sideliner SaaS?"
Q 06 · Competitive timing
"Two high-signal launches in Feb 2026 — Alvéole Aura and Biodiversity Lab. Does the market converging around this wedge mean we're late, or does it mean we're on time? The answer influences urgency."
Q 07 · Founder match
"This wedge rewards a hybrid of scientific credibility, enterprise sales, and data-platform engineering. Our team skews engineering. What's the gap and how do we close it — advisor, co-founder hire, or scoping the product tighter to what we can do?"
Ask · discussion

No decisions needed on these. The ask is honest input. Which of these questions do board members have strong opinions on? Which ones does the board want me to prioritize resolving before the Q3 reconvene?
