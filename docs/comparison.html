The trade, in one document

Old business. New proposition.
Side by side, honestly.

Both are real businesses. The question in front of the board isn't whether one is valid and the other isn't — it's whether we prefer a high-probability small outcome or a lower-probability large one. This document places them side by side across every dimension that matters, so the choice is legible.
Dimension
What we're comparing
Column A · status quo
The original business
Beekeeper mobile app + BLE sensors + SaaS subscription
Column B · proposition
The new proposition
Neutral data infrastructure for the pollination event
Customer
Individual beekeepers — hobbyist and sideliner. Managing 1–50 hives.
Enterprise buyers — parametric insurance MGAs, biodiversity consultancies, CPG sustainability teams, growers. Four convergent categories buying the same data.
User
The beekeeper — same as the customer.
Beekeepers and growers, instrumented. Customer and user are different people; user participation is the data-acquisition mechanism, not the revenue.
Product
Flutter mobile app + BLE hive sensors + cloud sync. Colony health tracking, alerts, logs.
Integrated per-site pollination record: hive telemetry + native pollinator acoustic + landscape phenology, with an API/dashboard for enterprise buyers and white-label reporting for consultancies.
Revenue model
SaaS subscription.
Enterprise data licensing + API access. Potential design-partner services in year one.
Deal size
~$120–$300 per user per year.
~$50K–$500K per enterprise contract (consultancy pilots at the low end, insurance data licenses at the high end).
Core data asset
Per-hive sensor readings, single-user, not aggregated.
Aggregated, multi-source, multi-site pollination record with re-licensing rights. The asset compounds; the original does not.
Defensibility
Product UX, workflow stickiness, switching cost.
Data network effects, aggregated rights, scientific methodology, integration partnerships (acoustic vendors, eDNA labs, satellite APIs).
Realistic revenue ceiling
$1–5M ARR. Proven out by BroodMinder, HiveTracks, ApisProtect, Pollenity, Arnia.
$50–250M ARR if two of the four buyer segments convert. Upside case references Floodbase ($17M raised, Swiss Re partner), Climate Corp ($1.1B exit), Alvéole Aura trajectory.
Closest precedent
BroodMinder, HiveTracks — capped lifestyle SaaS.
Floodbase (flood data vendor to reinsurers), Climate Corporation (weather data → agriculture), Alvéole Aura (biodiversity data → enterprise REITs).
Time to first revenue
3–6 months.
12–18 months. Longer because enterprise sales cycles are longer and at least one methodology publication or pilot case study is required to clear the insurance-grade rigor bar.
Capital to MVP
~$200K.
~$2–4M seed. Funds hardware, partner integrations, first pilot deployments, and a scientific-credibility hire or advisor.
Team fit
Strong. We are exactly this team.
Technical fit strong. Scientific-credibility and enterprise-sales fit is the gap — closable via advisor, methodology publication, or co-founder hire. Not closable via wishful thinking.
Competitive pressure
High. Five-plus direct competitors, all capped, all converging on the same features.
Moderate. No integrated multi-layer player exists today. Adjacent giants (BeeHero, NatureMetrics, Alvéole, AgriSound) each own one layer — none owns the integration.
Beachhead / GTM
App store launch, direct marketing to beekeeping community, conference presence.
Phase 0 validation (six weeks, 12 calls) → design-partner pilot with one consultancy or one MGA → expand instrumented footprint. Founder-sold, not ad-acquired.
Stack continuity
N/A — this is the existing stack.
BLE firmware as one data layer if it passes insurance-grade rigor in Phase 0. Cloud ingest carries forward. Beekeeper relationships carry forward. Stack is a tailwind, not the reason to pick this bet.
Successful outcome
Profitable small business, $2–5M ARR, possible acquihire exit at $2–10M.
Scientific-data-vendor business, $50–250M ARR, acquisition target for reinsurer, ag-insurance group, or AgTech strategic. Outcome range $100M–$1B+.
Failed outcome
Slow decline as beekeepers churn to free tools. Years of runway burned reaching a ceiling that was visible from day one.
Phase 0 kills the thesis cleanly in six weeks for $5K. Board decides: return capital, or run a value-first restart on a different market. No years lost.
Primary risk
The ceiling is the business. No amount of execution unlocks venture-scale. The risk is certainty of a small outcome, not probability of failure.
Buyer validation — will enterprises actually license pollination data at scale? Plus: 18-month runway to meaningful revenue, and a scientific-credibility gap to close.
Outcome distribution
Column A · original
$0 $5M $50M $500M $1B+
Narrow, peaked around a small-but-likely outcome. Low variance. Very high probability of some revenue. Near-zero probability of a venture outcome.
Column B · new proposition
$0 $5M $50M $500M $1B+
Wider. Meaningful mass at zero (Phase 0 kills the thesis) and meaningful right tail (scientific data vendor outcome). Lower probability of revenue, higher probability of a venture outcome if revenue appears at all.

The bottom line

We are not choosing between "a good business" and "a bad business." We are choosing between a near-certain small outcome and a less-certain large one, with a cheap six-week decision point in between that tells us which side of that trade to commit to.

Column A summary
High-probability, low-ceiling business.
Run it for ten years and you end with a lifestyle business. Real revenue, small outcome, no venture return. The risk here is certainty of a small result — not uncertainty of failure.
Column B summary
Lower-probability, higher-ceiling business.
Phase 0 tells us within six weeks whether the probability is high enough to commit $2–4M and 18 months. If killed, we're out six weeks and $5K. If validated, we're aimed at an outcome the original business couldn't produce at its best.
The actual question before the board tonight
Would we rather almost certainly land a small outcome, or plausibly land a large one — with a cheap, pre-committed way to know the difference in six weeks?
